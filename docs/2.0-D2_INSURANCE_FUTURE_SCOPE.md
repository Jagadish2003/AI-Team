# 2.0-D2 T5 — Insurance-specific detector needs: future-scope record

**Story:** 2.0-D2 (Insurance Template). **Task:** T5 — Record Insurance-specific
detector needs as future scope. **Type:** Design review / documentation.

This document is the **future-story record** the D2 story requires (AC4: *"Any
identified need for domain-specific detectors is recorded as future scope, not
implemented in-story."*). It runs the seeded Insurance estate through the
existing Service Cloud detector set, records which claims / underwriting /
policy-servicing patterns produce useful findings, and — for every genuine
domain-specific gap — captures a linkable future pack story with enough detail
to estimate later.

It exists so that a new detector, scorer, or Insurance pack is **not** silently
absorbed into what is a configuration-only story. Everything below the
"Implementation boundary" line is *recorded, not built*.

---

## 1. Implementation boundary (what 2.0-D2 does NOT do)

2.0-D2 adds **no** Insurance pack, Insurance scorer, Insurance detector module,
new detector dispatch branch, new ingestion surface, or domain-specific
scoring-engine logic. The Insurance template
(`backend/discovery/packs/template_registry.py` → `insurance`) continues to
reuse the already-registered `service_cloud` pack and its seven shipped
detectors. A structural test
(`backend/tests/contract/test_insurance_future_scope.py`) fails the build if any
Insurance detector, pack, scorer, ingest surface, or runner branch appears.

Every gap in §4 is a candidate for a **separate future pack story**, and must
not be implemented under D2 without its own approval and estimation.

---

## 2. Gap-assessment method

The seeded, insurance-shaped estate
(`backend/tests/contract/fixtures/insurance_estate_seed.json`) is
structurally identical to the offline Salesforce fixture, so it drives the
**real** Service Cloud detector inputs. All seven `service_cloud` detectors were
run against it and their findings recorded. Reproduce with:

```
cd backend
python -m pytest tests/contract/test_insurance_future_scope.py -k demonstrates -q
```

The assessment classifies each observed insurance workflow area as one of:

* **Covered** — an existing generic detector produces a useful, non-misleading
  finding; the operational shape is genuinely domain-agnostic.
* **Partial** — an existing detector fires and the finding is *true*, but the
  generic wording/criterion misses domain semantics that change how a
  practitioner would triage it.
* **Gap** — the pattern is not representable by the generic detectors at all
  (false negative), or the generic result would actively mislead, because the
  signal lives in Insurance-specific objects/semantics the shipped Service
  Cloud ingest never reads.

Each gap is further tagged with a **gap classification** distinguishing a true
**detector gap** from a **connector**, **focus**, **terminology**,
**configuration**, or **seed-quality** problem (see §5) — because only a detector
(and usually connector) gap justifies a future pack story; the others are
already-solved or out-of-band concerns.

## 4. Confirmed domain-specific gaps (future pack scope)

Each gap is a candidate pattern named in the D2 story. **Candidates only** — none
may be implemented under D2 without separate approval and estimation. Every gap
below is a **detector gap** and, because the signal lives in objects the shipped
Service Cloud ingest does not read, also a **connector gap** — i.e. a future
*pack* (ingest + detectors + scorer + terminology), on the proven FSC pattern.

### G1 — Coverage-determination loops

* **Business pattern:** repeated back-and-forth deciding whether a loss is
  covered — coverage questions bounced between adjuster, coverage counsel, and
  the policyholder, re-opening the same claim decision. Distinct from ordinary
  case rework because the *subject* is a coverage position, and each loop delays
  indemnity and inflates cycle time.
* **Required source objects:** `Claim`, `ClaimCoverage` / coverage-decision
  records, coverage-position status history (a Case owner-change count cannot
  see a coverage decision reversing).
* **Expected evidence:** count of coverage-position reversals per claim over the
  window, dwell between reversals, and the queues/roles the loop traverses —
  groups/queues only, never a named adjuster or claimant.
* **Privacy constraints:** claim coverage can reveal claimant circumstances
  (injury, loss type). Aggregate only; apply the 1.9 aggregation floor; never
  name an individual claimant or adjuster; redact free-text coverage rationale
  before indexing.
* **Detector criterion:** fire when coverage-position reversals per claim exceed
  a calibrated floor across a minimum claim volume, with per-claim (not global)
  baselining so a single complex claim does not fire.
* **Terminology:** coverage determination, coverage position, reservation of
  rights, indemnity.
* **Scoring considerations:** effort concentration (reversal count × dwell);
  regulated coverage decisions are the *least* automatable, so a coverage loop
  should rank as a triage/routing improvement, never as "automate the coverage
  decision."
* **Seed data:** claims with 3+ coverage-position reversals vs a control claim
  resolved in one pass; both above the minimum-volume guard.
* **Acceptance criteria:** detector fires on the seeded loop, stays silent on the
  single-pass control, carries the four-part contract, and names no individual.
* **Gap classification:** **Detector + connector gap.** Not terminology (T2 does
  not create the coverage object), not focus, not config, not seed-quality — the
  coverage-decision object does not exist in the Service Cloud ingest at all.

### G2 — Reserve-change approvals

* **Business pattern:** approvals to raise or lower a claim's financial reserve.
  `APPROVAL_BOTTLENECK` sees the *dwell*, but is blind to **materiality**: a slow
  approval on a $2M reserve increase is a different risk than a fast one on a
  $500 adjustment, and repeated reserve churn on one claim signals estimation
  weakness.
* **Required source objects:** `Claim`, reserve/financial-line records with
  reserve amounts and change deltas, reserve-approval history.
* **Expected evidence:** reserve-change dwell weighted by reserve delta, and
  reserve-churn count per claim — with amounts bucketed/aggregated, never a raw
  reserve figure tied to a claim id in output.
* **Privacy constraints:** reserve amounts are financially sensitive and
  market-moving in aggregate; bucket and aggregate; no per-claim monetary
  disclosure in narratives; aggregation floor applies.
* **Detector criterion:** fire on reserve-change approval dwell above a floor
  *scaled by reserve materiality*, or on reserve-churn count per claim above a
  floor — neither expressible from `ProcessInstance` dwell alone.
* **Terminology:** reserve, reserve adjustment, case reserve, IBNR, materiality.
* **Scoring considerations:** impact weighted by reserve materiality band, not
  raw dwell; automation shape favours routing/pre-fill, never the reserve
  decision itself.
* **Seed data:** a claim with repeated large reserve increases pending approval
  vs a control with a single immaterial adjustment.
* **Acceptance criteria:** fires on material reserve churn, silent on the
  immaterial control, four-part contract, no individual, no raw amount in copy.
* **Gap classification:** **Detector + connector gap.** The generic approval
  detector already covers the *operational* dwell (a partial), but reserve
  materiality is a genuine detector+object gap. Not terminology/focus/config/seed.

### G3 — Subrogation recovery handoffs

* **Business pattern:** handing a claim with recovery potential to a subrogation
  unit and pursuing recovery within a legal window. `HANDOFF_FRICTION` counts
  owner changes but cannot tell a *recovery-opportunity* handoff from ordinary
  routing, and a missed subrogation window is unrecovered money (leakage).
* **Required source objects:** `Claim`, subrogation/recovery records, recovery
  status and statute/recovery-window dates, the subrogation queue/unit.
* **Expected evidence:** claims with recovery potential not handed to
  subrogation before a window threshold, and subrogation-queue dwell — by
  group/queue only.
* **Privacy constraints:** recovery involves third parties and litigation;
  aggregate; redact counterparty/legal free text before indexing; no individual
  naming.
* **Detector criterion:** fire when recovery-eligible claims age past a
  recovery-window floor without a subrogation handoff, or when subrogation-queue
  dwell exceeds a floor — needs the recovery object and window dates.
* **Terminology:** subrogation, recovery, salvage, statute of limitations,
  recovery window.
* **Scoring considerations:** impact reflects recoverable value at risk; a missed
  window is high-impact; automation shape favours detection/routing, not the
  recovery decision.
* **Seed data:** recovery-eligible claims aging without handoff vs a control
  handed off promptly.
* **Acceptance criteria:** fires on aged un-handed recovery-eligible claims,
  silent on the promptly-handed control, four-part contract, no individual.
* **Gap classification:** **Detector + connector gap.** Not the generic handoff
  detector (which cannot distinguish recovery handoffs); not terminology/focus/
  config/seed.

### G4 — Underwriting-referral semantics (partial coverage → refinement)

* **Business pattern:** referrals to an underwriter for risk decisions outside a
  delegated authority — the referral *reason* (risk appetite, authority limit,
  accumulation) matters, not just the queue dwell. `APPROVAL_BOTTLENECK` /
  `PERMISSION_BOTTLENECK` already fire on the seed (6.5d / bottleneck 37.0), so
  the operational bottleneck **is covered**; what is missing is referral-reason
  and delegated-authority semantics that would separate "authority-limit"
  referrals from "risk-appetite" referrals.
* **Required source objects:** `Referral`/underwriting-referral records with
  referral reason and delegated-authority context (the generic Approval object
  has neither).
* **Expected evidence:** referral dwell and volume *segmented by referral
  reason*, and authority-limit breach frequency — by group/queue.
* **Privacy constraints:** may touch risk/rating factors; aggregate; no
  individual underwriter naming; aggregation floor.
* **Detector criterion:** fire on referral dwell/volume per referral-reason
  category above a floor — the generic approval detector has no reason
  dimension.
* **Terminology:** underwriting referral, risk appetite, delegated authority,
  referral reason, accumulation.
* **Scoring considerations:** an authority-limit referral is more automatable
  (rule-codifiable) than a judgment referral; scoring should reflect that split.
* **Seed data:** referrals split across reason categories with differing dwell.
* **Acceptance criteria:** segments referrals by reason and fires on the slow
  segment while the fast segment does not, four-part contract, no individual.
* **Gap classification:** **Partial — detector + connector refinement.** The
  operational bottleneck is already covered by shipped detectors (so this is
  *not* a false negative); the referral-reason segmentation is the additive
  detector+object gap. Not terminology/focus/config/seed.

### G5 — Claims leakage indicators

* **Business pattern:** money lost through claims handling — overpayment, missed
  recovery, incorrect reserve, duplicate payment, or benefits paid outside
  policy terms. This is the highest-value insurance pattern and is a **pure
  false negative**: it is structurally invisible to every generic detector
  because it lives in *payment / recovery / adjudication amounts* that the Case /
  Flow / Approval / NamedCredential inputs never carry.
* **Required source objects:** `Claim`, claim payment/transaction records,
  reserve records, recovery records, adjudication outcomes and policy limits.
* **Expected evidence:** aggregate leakage indicators — payment-vs-limit
  breaches, reserve-vs-paid variance, duplicate-payment signatures, missed-
  recovery rates — as **rates and aggregates only**, never a per-claim monetary
  finding.
* **Privacy constraints:** payment/adjudication data is highly sensitive
  (financial + potentially health/injury). Strict aggregation floor; no
  individual claimant/adjuster; no raw amount tied to a claim in output; redact
  adjudication free text before indexing.
* **Detector criterion:** fire on aggregate leakage-indicator rates above a
  calibrated floor across a minimum claim population — none of which is
  computable from the shipped inputs.
* **Terminology:** claims leakage, overpayment, indemnity leakage, recovery
  rate, adjudication accuracy.
* **Scoring considerations:** highest impact band (direct financial loss);
  automation shape favours detection/flagging for human review, never automated
  payment decisions (compliance guardrail).
* **Seed data:** a population with seeded overpayment / missed-recovery vs a
  clean control population.
* **Acceptance criteria:** fires on the leaky population, silent on the clean
  control, reports rates/aggregates only, four-part contract, no individual, no
  raw amount.
* **Gap classification:** **Detector + connector gap (pure false negative).**
  Explicitly **not** a seed-quality problem (the current seed intentionally does
  not carry payment objects — adding them is future-pack seed work), and not
  terminology/focus/config.

### G6 — Policy-renewal exceptions

* **Business pattern:** renewals that do not proceed cleanly — non-renewal,
  re-rating, referral-to-underwriting on renewal, lapse-and-reinstate.
  `REPETITIVE_AUTOMATION` counts servicing volume but cannot isolate the renewal
  *exception* path, which has renewal-cycle timing semantics (effective dates,
  renewal windows) absent from a Case volume count.
* **Required source objects:** `Policy`/`InsurancePolicy`, policy term and
  effective/expiry dates, renewal-transaction and renewal-status records.
* **Expected evidence:** renewal-exception rate and dwell relative to the renewal
  window, by product/segment — aggregate, no named policyholder.
* **Privacy constraints:** policy/renewal data can reveal policyholder segment;
  aggregate; no individual policyholder naming; aggregation floor.
* **Detector criterion:** fire on renewal-exception rate or pre-expiry dwell
  above a floor relative to the renewal window — needs policy term dates.
* **Terminology:** renewal, non-renewal, re-rating, lapse, reinstatement,
  renewal window, effective date.
* **Scoring considerations:** impact reflects retained-premium at risk; timing
  is critical (a missed renewal window is a lost policy); automation favours
  early flagging/routing.
* **Seed data:** policies approaching expiry with exception paths vs clean
  auto-renewals.
* **Acceptance criteria:** fires on the exception population within the renewal
  window, silent on clean auto-renewals, four-part contract, no individual.
* **Gap classification:** **Detector + connector gap.** Not terminology/focus/
  config/seed — the Policy object and term dates are absent from the Service
  Cloud ingest.

## 5. Explicitly recorded as NOT requiring additional detection

Recorded so a reviewer can see the boundary was drawn deliberately, not by
omission. These are **not** detector gaps:

* **Operational bottlenecks, handoffs, repetition, integration fan-in,
  knowledge linkage, cross-system duplication** — already covered by the seven
  shipped detectors (§3). No new detection needed.
* **Terminology** (policyholder, policy, service request, underwriting review,
  coverage requirement, claims queue) — a **configuration** concern, already
  delivered by 2.0-D2 T2 on the template. Not a detector gap.
* **Focus emphasis** — the inability of one canonical focus to emphasise all
  Insurance areas is a **focus-model** concern, already reported by 2.0-D2 T3
  (`metadata.focus_limitation`). Nothing is suppressed; only relative ranking
  differs. Not a detector gap.
* **Threshold calibration for insurance** — the shipped detectors' thresholds
  are Service-Cloud-general, not insurance-calibrated. The seed is *authored*,
  not captured from a real insurance org, so it proves the detectors *run*, not
  that any threshold is right for insurance. This is a **seed-quality /
  calibration** concern for the future pack's own calibration work — **not** a
  missing-detector gap.
* **Connector auth / ingestion of Case, Flow, ApprovalProcess, NamedCredential**
  — already shipped (the Service Cloud ingest). The *additional* Insurance
  objects (Claim, Policy, reserve, subrogation, payment) are the connector half
  of the future-pack gaps in §4, not a separate standalone connector story.

## 6. Future story record — "Insurance Discovery Pack" (D2-FS1)

The six gaps in §4 roll up into one linkable future story. It is **recorded, not
scheduled**; it carries no estimate here and must be approved and estimated
separately (the D2 story forbids absorbing it).

* **Story id:** `D2-FS1` — Insurance Discovery Pack (future).
* **Shape:** an Insurance domain pack on the proven FSC pattern — its own ingest
  (Claim / Policy / reserve / subrogation / payment objects), its own detectors
  (G1–G6), its own scorer calibration, and its own terminology — registered on
  the existing pack framework with zero engine changes, exactly as FSC was.
* **Depends on:** a connector surface for the Insurance objects above (Financial
  Services Cloud / Insurance data model or custom objects), and insurance-shaped
  seed data carrying those objects.
* **Privacy posture:** claims data is PII/PHI- and financially sensitive; the
  pack inherits the 1.9 aggregation floor and the no-individual-naming and
  no-automated-decision guardrails throughout.
* **Non-goal for D2:** none of D2-FS1 is built in 2.0-D2. D2 ships the template,
  terminology, focus defaults, honest anchoring, and this record — configuration
  only.
